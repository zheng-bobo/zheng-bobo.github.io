Summary: Resist the influence of external compliance forces and internal self-centered thoughts, and be wary of viewpoints that make you comfortable, that are in line with your own interests, and that satisfy your own selfish needs.

2. Several stages of becoming an excellent thinker:

1. Reckless thinker: never thinks about his own thinking process, does not analyze and evaluate his own way of thinking
               Only make decisions and give feedback to others without serious reflection
  2. Questioning thinkers: able to notice problems in our thinking process
                Trying to analyze and evaluate your own thinking
                In exploring those thinking structures that can generate thinking (such as: concepts, assumptions, inferences, meanings, opinions)
                Consider qualities that optimize thinking (clarity, accuracy, precision, relevance, logic)
                Discover self-deception in your own thinking
  3. Junior thinker: Analyze the logic of situations or problems
               Express issues clearly and accurately
               Check information for accuracy and relevance
               Distinguish between original information and other people's descriptions of the event
               Find out the assumptions that lead to your conclusions
               Identify biased beliefs, unfair conclusions, incorrectly used words, and ignored meanings
               Notice when our self-interest affects our perspective
      Pay attention to two points
      Dogmatic absolutism - the belief that true ideas are obtained not through reasoning and investigation, but through some preset, irrational beliefs.
      Subjective relativism - the belief that there are no standards of thinking for judging what is right or wrong.
  4. Thinker in practice: Prepare a plan - test the plan during execution - find an effective plan (not controlled by emotions)
                Take advantage of “wasted” time
                Solve a problem every day
                Internalize thinking standards
                Keep a thinking diary
                Practice thinking strategies
                Reshape your character
                change your self
                Change the way you look at things
                Pay attention to your emotions
                Analyze the impact of groups on your life

## Understand the nature of thinking and the basic mechanism of the mind

Nature of thinking: Self-centered
      The tendency to be dominated by self-centered thoughts and feelings is one of the most fundamental challenges humans face in learning. Our desires, pains, thoughts, and feelings deeply dominate our lives. We seek self-gratification out of an inherently selfish nature. --> You can start by cultivating a habit. From this process, you can experience the higher level of self-satisfaction brought by small achievements after boring and boring persistence.

      We rarely ask whether our own views are accurate, yet assume we have already thought them through. We avoid examining personal growth, our weaknesses, biases, and self-deception. Instead, we often seek to satisfy our own needs, avoid disagreement, and rationalize our views and behavior.

Three basic functions of the brain: thinking, feeling, and needs
               That is, analysis (What is this thing?) and cause and effect (What does this thing have to do with me?)

               Positive feelings are affecting thinking and needs (thinking mathematics is beautiful will affect your attitude towards learning mathematics)

               How can we cultivate positive feelings? Connect what we learn with our own lives. When academic ideas gain positive meaning in everyday life, they can unlock our motivation to learn and think.

               Try to understand the role and influence of a discipline's way of thinking, for example through the history of that discipline.
